Why the job starts in the yard.

A rat is a creature of habit and runs the same path night after night. It follows foundations, fences and the edge of a stone wall, and it digs where soil is soft and cover is thick. By the time you hear it inside, the route outdoors is already worn in.

Properties around Blauvelt vary a great deal, from compact village-style lots to deep ones that back onto trees. Hedged as always, the pattern is that more cover near the house means more places for a burrow to hide. We look at that cover before anything else.

Reading the grounds.

A perimeter walk takes about as long as a cup of coffee and yields most of the plan. We look for smooth, greasy paths along wall bases, fresh soil beside a step, gnawed fruit under a tree and droppings around feeders or compost.

  • Burrow mouths, usually the size of a golf ball or larger, with smooth packed edges.
  • Runways through leaf litter or along the base of a fence.
  • Gnaw marks on garage door seals, vent covers and shed corners.
  • Food sources such as seed, pet bowls, open bins and compost.

Callers sometimes place themselves by Blauvelt State Park or Veterans Memorial Park. We have no more information about either than their names, so tell us the street and what your own lot looks like. Wooded edges are worth mentioning, because they change where the first traps go.

Trapping and the long game.

Rat traps sit in enclosed boxes along the lanes, so the animal travels through a bottleneck and pets cannot reach the mechanism. We log each location and revisit on days we state in advance, since trap shy rats can take a few nights to commit.

Trapping alone does not end the story. Gaps in the foundation, loose vent covers and gnawed soffits are sealed with heavier materials than a mouse job needs. Our exclusion work covers the metals and sealants, and outdoor rodent control covers what to change in the yard.

What not to do first.

Poison scattered around the yard is a poor opening move. It can reach pets and wildlife, and a poisoned rat may die somewhere you cannot reach. The EPA page linked below explains why consumer bait now comes in locked stations.

Leave burrows undisturbed until we have seen them. Filling a hole without a plan sends the animal to dig a new one, often closer to the house.

After the first week.

Expect fewer droppings, fewer fresh paths and traps that move from busy to quiet. If activity continues, we recheck for an entry we missed, and we say so plainly. Bring up any change you notice, such as a new burrow after a storm.

Easy steps for today.

  • A car with debris under the hood should stay parked until someone has checked it.
  • Lawn seed, kibble and birdseed move indoors for the time being.
  • Anything chewed goes in a bag for the technician to see.
  • Find your breaker panel, in case a cable has been gnawed.

Before you call, deal with droppings the wet way

What follows restates advice from public health agencies. It suits any household, with or without a booked visit.

  • Brooms and vacuums are the wrong tools, because both stir settled droppings into the air where you breathe them.
  • The CDC suggests thirty minutes of airing for a closed space, and only after that does the cleaning begin.
  • You can avoid skin contact with droppings, urine and nesting scraps if you glove up first.
  • Wet every pellet and stain all the way through, then give the liquid time to work.
  • Towels and carcass can share one knotted bag, and thin plastic calls for a second bag around it.
  • Medical questions are outside what we can answer. Feeling unwell after contact with droppings is a reason to see a clinician.

Rat queries from nearby callers.

What separates a rat from a mouse?

Start with size. A rat pellet runs as long as a big rice grain or longer, whereas a mouse pellet is thinner and shorter. Heavy thumping overhead suggests something larger than a mouse.

Do stone walls cause rat problems?

A wall does not cause rats, but gaps between stones can shelter them and hide burrow entrances. Clearing brush along the wall and watching for fresh soil helps more than demolition.

Will rats leave on their own?

Rarely. As long as a meal, a warm spot and a doorway are on offer, they remain, and unsealed gaps invite a return.

Is trapping safe around dogs and children?

We use enclosed, tamper-resistant boxes where the animals travel, and we tell you every location. Keep pets away from them regardless.

Can rats come through a toilet or drain?

That does happen in some buildings, but we do not assume it. The inspection checks plumbing paths along with every other entry before we say anything about yours.
